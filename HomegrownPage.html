Real Roots x Homegrown

In collaberation with Nina Cohen and Isabel Sanchez.

Home Grown is a speculative redesign of the Santiago Bernabéu Stadium that reimagines its underground hypogeum as agricultural infrastructure. The project explores how spaces designed for entertainment and consumption could instead contribute to food production, resource conservation, and urban resilience in response to climate change and growing concerns around food security.

Project Brief: The project began with a detailed reconstruction of the Santiago Bernabéu Stadium, requiring a deep understanding of its architecture, operations, and recent redevelopment. Through this process, we became interested in the stadium's hypogeum, a highly engineered underground system used to store and move the playing field.

Rather than using this technology solely to support entertainment, we explored how similar infrastructure could be repurposed to address future environmental challenges. With projections indicating increasing desertification across Spain and growing pressure on global food systems, the project asks how existing architecture might contribute to food production instead of consumption.

Process

After digitally modelling the stadium and studying its infrastructure, we developed a speculative agricultural system capable of operating at multiple scales. The proposal transforms the hypogeum into a controlled growing environment that supports year-round food production while making efficient use of limited space and resources. The system was then adapted across three scales: the home, where families could grow essential produce; the stadium, where agriculture becomes a public and educational experience; and the city, where large-scale production helps reduce dependence on imported food and vulnerable supply chains. The design combines research into climate change, urban agriculture, and resource efficiency with architectural speculation to imagine alternative futures for existing infrastructure.

Home Grown proposes a future in which architecture plays a more active role in supporting local food systems. While speculative, the project is grounded in current environmental challenges and explores how existing technologies and spaces might be repurposed to build resilience in a changing climate. More than a redesign of a stadium, the project became an exploration of how architecture can evolve alongside the needs of society, transforming spaces built for consumption into systems that actively support communities.

This project was a daunting exercise in research and architectural thinking. The key idea was understnding unintended or ignored concequences, which meant the final piece had to reflect that we understood the entire ecosystem and environment in which it would exist. While this project is highly speculative it relied on real data, floorplans and technological capabilites.
